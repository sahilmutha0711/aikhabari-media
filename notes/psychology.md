## CURRENT TOOLKIT (use in every script)
1. Autopilot breaker: the first line must contradict a belief the viewer holds, or reveal a stake for THEM ("Everyone on H-1B thinks they get 60 days... A new rule wants to make it zero.").
2. Social currency: give one fact their friends don't know yet, or a ready-made opinion. People share what makes them look smart/funny/informed.
3. Re-commit beat within 3-6 s of the hook: a second reason to stay ("Watch till the end, number 3 is the one nobody's talking about" / "Save this, you'll need it on Nov 10").
4. Open loops every 8-10 s: numbered items ("rule two... rule three is the quiet one"), "and that's not even the worst part", a question you answer later.
5. Pattern interrupt every 2-3 s on screen (new visual, number pop, cut) and a tone change in the voice at the rehook.
6. Payoff last, end right after it; last line loops back to the hook word so replays feel natural.
7. Earn the send, don't demand it: make one line so useful that it names who needs it ("If you know someone on OPT, this is the date they need"). Instagram said it won't recommend content it believes explicitly asks for shares, comments or tags to "hack" reach; it called open-ended questions and CTAs fine. Use one open question ("what would you do?") over "comment YES" or a keyword. Like-asks stay rare, and only as the owner's call (max 2 engagement lines per Reel, playbook 00d).
8. Score each Reel on Mosseri's top three signals: average watch time, sends per reach, likes per reach. Sends matter slightly more for reaching non-followers, so judge growth Reels on sends per reach, not likes.
9. Write for sound-off scrollers too, and keep text readable: frame-1 text must state the stake on its own in at most 5 words (a 7-word line takes about 3 s to read), live captions in 2-3-word groups, and B-roll cards show only the key word or number, never a second sentence next to the captions. Keep captions on; spell visa and tech terms right.

### 2026-11-05: Instagram's own ranking guidance and what it says about asks
Source quality: Mosseri and Instagram quotes below are as reported by Social Media Today, not read on Instagram directly. Instagram's own ranking explainer could not be opened in this run, and Meta's Transparency Center pages returned no body text.

**Findings**
1. Mosseri (Jan 2025): "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends." He pointed creators to average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
   - Apply: for growth, the story needs a clear person who needs it. Saves are not in that top three, so playbook 00's "saves second" is our own priority, not Mosseri's stated ranking.
   - Lines (fill [ ] with verified facts only): "If you're on OPT, the number that matters here is [verified number]." / "Know someone on H-1B? This is the date they need: [verified date]."
2. Instagram (2024): "If we believe your content explicitly asks for engagement through shares, comments, tags, or other actions, then we won't recommend it." Its example was asking people to comment a specific word, number or emoji. [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-says-using-certain-ctas-can-impact-post-reach/717732/)
3. Instagram then clarified: "There haven't been changes to our recommendation guidelines." The test it gave is intent: it is engagement bait "if the end goal is to simply maximize the quantity of engagements as a way to 'hack' reach." "Open-ended questions and calls-to-action are a great way to engage with your audience." [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-advice-single-word-ctas-longer-reels/718151/)
   - Limits: the original video was later deleted, the statements are from 2024, and nothing public shows how a spoken ask is detected. Treat this as a risk to manage, not a proven penalty.
   - Apply: this weakens the old toolkit claim that save/send asks are safe. Our "send this to..." triggers, "YES/NO" comment asks and "like this video" lines are all explicit asks, so they carry some of this risk. "Watch till the end" asks for no share, comment or tag, and it was in our best-held Reel (xAI).
   - Lines: "Would you take that deal? Tell me why." / "Who actually wins here? I want to hear your reasoning." / "I think [labelled take]. Change my mind."
4. Instagram's retention chart shows the percentage of views still watching at each moment; skip rate is reported as the share leaving in the first three seconds (secondary definition, not an Instagram quote). [Website Builder Expert](https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/news/instagram-reel-retention-chart/)
   - Apply: use the chart to place open loops. Before scripting, note where the last Reel's curve dropped, then put the next reveal just ahead of that point.
   - Re-commit lines: "But that's the small number. The one that affects you is next." / "Here's where it gets strange."

### 2026-11-06: On-screen text, sound-off viewing and reading speed
Source quality: survey numbers are self-reported and partly vendor-commissioned; the reading-speed and redundancy findings are peer-reviewed or textbook research on subtitles and instructional video, not on Reels, so they transfer by analogy. Netflix's subtitle style guide could not be opened in this run, so no Netflix figure is used.

**Findings**
1. Many people scroll with the sound off. Verizon Media + Publicis Media (online survey, 5,616 US adults aged 18-54, April 2019): 69% watch video with sound off in public, 25% even in private; 80% of people who use captions are not hearing impaired. [Streaming Media](https://www.streamingmedia.com/Articles/News/Online-Video-News/80-of-Video-Caption-Users-Arent-Hearing-Impaired-Finds-Verizon-131860.aspx). Coverage of the same study also reports that 80% say they are more likely to watch a whole video when captions are available (self-report, not measured completion). [Next TV](https://nexttv.com/news/mobile-videos-often-watched-without-audio-study-finds). Counterpoint: TikTok's own Nielsen study (2020, platform-commissioned) says 88% of TikTok users call sound "vital to the TikTok experience." [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/evolution-of-sound-volume-1)
   - Apply: write every Reel for both kinds of viewer. The sound-off scroller decides from the frame-1 text alone, so that text must carry the stake on its own and must not only make sense once you hear the voice ("This rule could cut your 60 days", not "Wait for it..."). Sound-on viewers still get the voice, music and tone change.
   - Frame-1 text examples (fill [ ] with verified facts only): "H-1B layoff? [N] days, not 60" / "OpenAI just [verified action]" / "Your OPT clock: [verified change]".
2. When text moves too fast, people stop reading it fully and understand less. Kruger, Wisniewska & Liao (Applied Psycholinguistics, 2021) tested subtitles at 12, 20 and 28 characters per second: comprehension fell as speed rose, and faster subtitles were read less completely, with fewer words reread. [Cambridge Core](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/journals/applied-psycholinguistics/article/why-subtitle-speed-matters-evidence-from-word-skipping-and-rereading/A7DB0CBF5910353143738CF9C1721317)
   - Our own math (not from the paper): a 7-word hook is about 35-45 characters, so it takes about 3 s to read at 12-15 cps. That is longer than our 2-second hook window. Harry and the owner talk at about 3 words/s (about 16-18 cps), so live captions are already near the fast end.
   - Apply: frame-1 hook text should be at most 5 words (about 25 characters), or stay on screen for at least 3 s. Keep live captions in 2-3-word groups. In the fastest stretch (numbers, lists), slow the voice slightly or put the number alone on screen.
3. Repeating every spoken word as on-screen text on top of a busy graphic can hurt understanding. Clark & Mayer, e-Learning and the Science of Instruction, ch. 7, the redundancy principle: "In general, do not add printed text to a narrated graphic." Its boundary conditions say the extra text can help when there is no graphic, when the pace is slow, when the narration uses technical or unfamiliar words, or when the text is shorter than the narration (key words). [O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/e-learning-and-the/9781119158660/c07.xhtml)
   - Limits: this is learning research in a classroom-style setting. Sound-off viewers on Instagram need captions anyway, so this does NOT mean dropping captions.
   - Apply: during B-roll, let the card show only the key word or number ("60 → 0 DAYS", "$6.3B"), not a second full sentence next to the captions. Our audience hears technical terms (OPT, STEM extension, I-140) and many are non-native English speakers, which is the boundary case where captions help, so keep captions on and spell those terms correctly.
   - Re-commit/open-loop lines that read well as captions (short, one idea each): "That's the small change. The big one is next." / "Now look at this number." / "Here's who it hits first."

**Our metrics on this** (notes/metrics.md): Binance (11-03) opened on a busy split screen with "Okay, this is..." and had 76% skip. Midterms (11-04) had one clear audience in the hook and 59% skip. This fits "one idea and readable text in frame 1", but the topic and the opening line also changed, so it is not proof.

**Test next:** for the next 3 Reels, cap the frame-1 text at 5 words, show only the key word or number on B-roll cards (no sentence cards), and keep captions in 2-3-word groups. Compare skip rate against the recent median of about 60% (Midterms 59.8%, NY 60.3%, Ambani 58.6%) and average watch time against about 16 s. Log the frame-1 text and its word count in each metrics row so we can compare.
